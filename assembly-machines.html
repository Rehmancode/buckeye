Where it fits

Assembly machines suit small parts made in volume, where manual assembly is slow and inconsistent. We have built machines and lines for dry cell batteries, flashlights, locks, ball pens, scooter parts and clutch parts, from single stations to complete lines.

Our dry cell battery line shows how far this goes: three interconnected machines controlled by PLC, with rotary dial feeding, part release devices and feeding conveyors between machines. It runs 300 batteries a minute at 94% efficiency with one operator.

How a project runs

  1. Study. We note the operations, part size and configuration, production rate, plant arrangement and length of production runs.
  2. Concept. The basic machine characteristics and process layout are set from those needs and your company’s experience.
  3. Build. Standard parts are used from concept to manufacture for trouble-free operation.
  4. Trials. The machine or line is run and checked against your production target.
  5. Delivery and service. We deliver, commission and support the line.

Frequently asked questions

What assembly rate can you achieve?

It depends on the part and the process. Our dry cell battery line runs at 300 batteries per minute with 94% efficiency and one operator. Tell us your target rate and we will say what is achievable.

Do you build automatic and semi-automatic machines?

Both. We design, build and test automatic and semi-automatic assembly machines and complete assembly lines.

Do you handle part feeding?

Yes. Feeding is designed into the machine, using devices such as rotary dials and feeding conveyors between machines.

Next step: share your desired production rate, relevant part drawings and any machine concept. Our application engineers will review them and show how they can be achieved, without obligation.
